Chains of Glory

Micah had a shrine of his own; he made an ephod and some household idols, and he appointed one of his sons as his priest. At that time Israel had no king, and everyone did whatever they wanted.
Judges 17:5-6

Yes, Micah is a bit like all those who "have no master" and invent anything at all to live. Micah stole money from his mother, eleven hundred pieces of silver to be exact; his mother then cursed the thief, but the thief was in her own house. Micah confessed to his mother that he was the thief and gave the money back. To settle the curse, the mother took two hundred pieces of silver from that money and had a silversmith make a statue covered in silver.

Without going into detail: the mother curses the thief, so she has cursed her own son; she tries to take the situation back in hand and, according to biblical truth, she also put him in a bad position before God, because she had an idol made for him, a statue to worship. If we go back in our memories, everyone remembers the commandment that says: do not worship other gods.

What condemnation can we give Micah, and what can we give his mother?

Well, none, and not because I am good: at the start there is a fundamental point that says: Israel had no king, and everyone did whatever they wanted.

Who is the last King of Israel still alive whom we can recognise and follow as a guide? (I am sure there would be seconds, if not minutes, of panic before the answer.)

Jesus.

Then the glory of the Lord will be revealed, and all people will see it. The Lord himself has promised this." A voice cries out, "Proclaim a message!" "What message shall I proclaim?" I ask. "Proclaim that all people are like grass; they last no longer than wild flowers. Grass withers and flowers fade, when the Lord sends the wind blowing over them. People are no more enduring than grass. Yes, grass withers and flowers fade, but the word of our God endures for ever."
Isaiah 40:5-8

If we do not become aware that we are grass that withers, we will not realise that we are destined to rot on this earth; but if, instead of idolising people, things or animals, we establish a king in our life, then our life is saved.
I deliberately wrote "a king" to point to one specific king, because in the end everyone follows their own king, but no one becomes aware of the only King, the King of kings (notice the nuance of the capital and small letters).

The one who created the earth sits on his throne above the dome of the sky; from up there people look like ants. He stretched out the skies like a curtain, he spread them out like a tent to live in. He brings down powerful rulers and reduces the princes of the earth to nothing. They are like young plants, just set out and barely rooted; when the Lord sends a wind, they dry up and blow away like straw. "To whom can the holy God be compared? Is there anyone else like him?" Look up at the sky! Who created the stars you see? Only one, the Mighty and Powerful. He knows how many there are and calls each one by name! And not one of them is ever missing.
Isaiah 40:22-26

Is there any need to add anything?

He is the King, He is God, but not just any god: God the Father, Father of Jesus, the last King of Israel, still alive. Yes, He is risen, right?

He strengthens those who are weak and tired. Even those who are young grow weak; young people can fall exhausted. But those who trust in the Lord for help will find their strength renewed. They will rise on wings like eagles; they will run and not get weary; they will walk and not grow weak.
Isaiah 40:29-31

Does He give superpowers?

God regenerates us if we plug ourselves into the socket called Jesus (if we want to use a modern, electrical term).

So we don't need human chains, or chains tied to things like statues, objects like four-leaf clovers, lucky horn charms or magic pendants?

Of course not.

We write to you about the Word of life, which has existed from the very beginning. We have heard it, and we have seen it with our eyes; yes, we have seen it, and our hands have touched it. When this life became visible, we saw it; so we speak of it and tell you about the eternal life which was with the Father and was made known to us. What we have seen and heard we announce to you also, so that you will join with us in the fellowship that we have with the Father and with his Son Jesus *Christ. We write this in order that our joy may be complete.
1 John 1:1-4

So the word matters; that is, the way we pronounce our words matters. It mattered that Micah's mother did not curse the thief, even though he had taken her money. Micah's mother did not know the thief was her son, but her son, for his part, did not know that worshipping statues was wrong (even though he knew the Old Testament); but it was not his fault, he did not know whom to follow. In fact Micah later loses everything, if you read on in the story.
The apostle Thomas was a bit sceptical, but Jesus told him to put his finger in His wound, and so Thomas believed. Jesus answered that Thomas was blessed because he had believed by seeing and touching, but far more blessed would be those who believed without having seen, that is, all of us.

I believe in the last King of Israel because I believe in the Word. In reality I believe there are many words spoken at random, or words drawn from the original one, the Bible. I believe in this Word because I hold it to be the Word from the beginning, which I could not hear, which I could not "see", but which today I can contemplate and touch with my own hands. Above all because I realise that I am like grass that withers, but which in its life cycle can produce a beautiful flower; I am also an ant on this earth, but God is the dome of this world.

I don't want to open a political or religious debate, but there are already political domes that want to crush us like ants, and many domes that want to set themselves up over the church of Christ; yet we are the bricks scattered across the earth that together form the walls of Christ's church, and He is the dome from which He stretches out a curtain, spreading it out like a tent to live in.
Whether we want to believe it or not, He is above every earthly dome or earthly chain, and to Him we can attribute a true Kingdom on this earth; and with that we can speak of Him to create communion (the sharing of the same real right, usually of ownership, among several people; fellowship. The community of those who belong to the same religious confession).

We are blades of grass swaying on this earth, and we live by His breath (the Holy Spirit). All the gratitude that binds us to God goes to Jesus, and our bond belongs to Jesus alone. For the rest, we only wither and we DIE TWICE over.

If, then, we say that we have fellowship with him, yet at the same time live in the darkness, we are lying both in our words and in our actions. But if we live in the light, just as he is in the light, then we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus, his *Son, purifies us from every sin. If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and there is no truth in us. But if we confess our sins to God, he will keep his promise and do what is right: he will forgive us our sins and purify us from all our wrongdoing. If we say that we have not sinned, we make a liar out of God, and his word is not in us.
1 John 1:6-10

All clear? By the way, I highlighted a verse, verse 9 to be exact, which in this interconfessional version of the Bible says: If we openly confess our sins, He will forgive them. I want to point out this verse not because it is better than the others or because I am attached to it in particular, but because it is more useful than the others, since it makes us aware that we must acknowledge our sins. So I ask myself: if I acknowledge my sins, I know that I am wrong or have been wrong, and this, in any case, puts me in communion with God. Having a sin does not exclude me from communion with God. By acknowledging this, I recognise the sin, and my will to have a relationship with God will certainly lead me to improve my condition. I am sure of the Word of God, which says that He will free us from all our wrongdoing because He is GOOD.

The sinner will never be good like God; man has sinned and will surely keep on sinning. Jesus was a man, but His perfection does not belong to man, because man remains a sinner.

Can this stir up controversy? Of course!

So let me give an example: can a man who does not know the love of God the Father, nor the events of Christ on earth, who listens to Jesus made man, Perfect and a model to follow, identify himself with perfection? Of course not. I believe that any man must become aware of his sin, because it is by acknowledging it that he will draw closer to improvement. It is the will to improve that will bring him closer to Jesus, but this man, I am sure, will not reach perfection. God created everything perfect, but man complicated it, and it is precisely our desire to make something good blossom in our lives that will allow us not to die like simple grass of the field, but with a glory of splendour of Light.

I don't know if I've made myself clear.

Have a good reflection.
