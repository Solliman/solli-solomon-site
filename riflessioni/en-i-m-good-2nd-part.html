I'm Good (2nd part)


To better understand this article, if you haven't already, I invite you to read the first part, where I mentioned some of the habits we often rely on to "seem" good, by clicking (you can find that article among the other reflections in the archive).
Why seem good and not really be good?
Why keep mind and heart, reason and feeling, far apart? 
Why not look for a balance that feeds mind and heart in the same way?!
Maybe you think it is automatic to feed mind and heart with the same thing, or maybe you have never noticed the distance between the action and the intention.


How many times have you given a gift  only because the occasion forced you to, maybe to someone you didn't even like?
There you go! Your action was good, but it is the intention (which cannot be seen) that gives value to everything you are and do.

The gift story is an example to show how, in reality, not everything we do is tied to good intentions.
Covering a wrong intention with a good action will not make you feel well; you need to connect the two, and that requires sacrifice.
Maybe that is why you have never stopped collecting "patchwork relationships" that have never valued who you really are AND what you could give to others.

You have value, a value you cannot let be eaten away by the distance between mind and heart.
If you feel you cannot get along with someone, just express yourself in the right way and draw clearly visible boundaries in the relationship, instead of building relationships of convenience or "leech" relationships.

What is a leech relationship?

A leech relationship is one held together by one person's interest in being constantly listened to and encouraged with positivity, exploiting the patience and kindness of the other.

Emotional support in a relationship is important but it is important to recognise the right moment to "unload" your burdens on the person next to you, to understand that it is not always the right time, that you cannot always play the victim, that a good part of our problems depends on what we bring about with our words, that the support we receive (if we consider it such) must be held close to our heart, that the person listening to us may well be tired of hearing our repeated complaints.


If you recognised yourself in this description of a "leech relationship", good! Decide to be less selfish, learn to listen to the other person too, and learn to be the supporter rather than the supported: talk about your victories as well as your defeats, and if you cannot see yourself as a winner, I invite you to reflect on this sentence, which has taught me a great deal too:

"Come to me, all of you who are tired from carrying heavy loads, and I will give you rest.
Take my yoke and put it on you, and learn from me, because I am gentle and humble in spirit; and you will find rest..."
Matthew 11:28-29


The true peace you are looking for is in Jesus; no human being can give it to you. They may listen to you, give you advice, comfort you for a moment, but the one who will always be ready to listen to you, the one who can truly heal your weaknesses and wounds, the one who can help you close the gap between action and intention will always and only be Him, Jesus. The choice is yours!

Thank you for reading.
