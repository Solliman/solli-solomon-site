The heights of life

How can we not consider what surrounds us, what we observe, what makes us connect the facts of the past with the present.
I cannot help doing it, because I am a person who, with time and with the life I have left behind, has begun to weigh things a great deal, even going beyond what lies behind whatever presents itself naturally.

An example to better explain what I am writing is a day trip out of town when my wife and I stopped to look at a beautiful village perched on the sheer face of a mountain. Our thought was that, in the years when that mountain village was conceived, the intention was surely to create a safe world, far from the fear of an attack or an invasion.
Today this need certainly no longer exists, because what develops in our lives has more to do with well-being and lifestyle than with isolation, even though the fears remain the same.

It is hard to explain the subtle state of being and of life that was created and lived back then compared with the transformation of today's reality. From vertical growth to horizontal growth.
As I write, a thousand other thoughts on this are being set free, namely the development of different lifestyles in these two ways, vertical and horizontal, while keeping the fears.

Fears are rooted in man; everyone is afraid of something. Fear also leads us to attach ourselves to wrong things, and wrong things, when they become a lifestyle, are hardly ever recognised and hide inside something that looks "good".
Being afraid of going hungry because you have to rush to work, and grabbing a sandwich at McDonald's, cannot lead to a healthy diet if it happens all the time. It is obviously just an example to relate fear to a wrong habit.

Another example could be thinking that going to church every Sunday can settle our conscience or fix our life.
Regarding this last example, I also connected it to the building of that little village on the mountain: on one side the fear of something, on the other the desire to reach the sky. How can I not think of the Tower of Babel.

Man needs to reach heights, needs to come out of his fears, to emerge, to place himself above others to keep everything in order, under control, and why not, to become an authority, a symbol, a flag that flies high but only follows the Wind.
The Wind is a clear biblical reference, described in a verse as something that blows wherever it pleases, though no one knows where it comes from.
It may seem a complex thought, but I think of all this humanity we live in today, in this 2023, which is no different at all from previous years, and which is still leading us nowhere except to live more and more absurdity and contradiction.

There is no one left who goes against the current. Perhaps the only ones who went against the current were the apostles, and maybe not all of them, maybe two, maybe one, and the fact is that only they truly lost their lives to say that there is something different.
Today churches have become clubs; there are many of them, in different styles, all representing something good, they are all excellent, and many members are very attached to them because they truly live Christ, but what they live is simply a system that leads you to be evangelised and then involved in many events and meetings to reach depth with Christ.

It is wonderful to deal with people who truly live Christ, with those who have a good heart and live according to a biblical concept, but I do not believe that in Italy the style of all of us is to have a great culture. By culture I mean one's own heritage that comes from experience and study. In Italy our culture stops at dialects, at knowing our origins, at pagan festivals, at village customs, but these remain only things handed down. Few people grow their culture in anything, and I am certainly not the one saying it. Just think that we are not a people who know a second language; or rather, we know the dialect of our own town and our second language is Italian. We are a country of hearsay, in the sense that if I hear that "that thing" can be done because "my friend said so", then it can be done.

All this to say that, although communities grow and there is a heart for God bound to Jesus the Christ, not everyone grows in biblical culture by reading the Bible; they follow the psalmist of the day instead.
Is it good, is it bad, is it fine, is it not fine? The fact is that the rule is "it's fine by me, let's love each other", the captain of the day said that if I go there and do this, I am doing well, "my friend said so", so it can be done.

But if the Bible says that each of us who openly and publicly acknowledges (now don't start analysing that little word) Jesus as Lord and Saviour is a brick that makes up the church on earth, then to this day I cannot explain why there are so many church denominations. Isn't the church of Christ one and only? Is the bride one, or is the church Muslim so that it can have many brides?

If it is true that we are the bricks that make up the church, a psalm says that God is the dome of this church.
Now think of a house with four sides, exposed to the four winds and closed by a single roof. I ask you (because I have already asked myself): are the bricks of the north side joined to those of the east side, and therefore to the south side, and so on with the west side?

Of course! The cement that holds them together is Jesus Christ. We are not talking about a thousand houses but about one, unique, single house, made up of all of us who believe in Christ, who do not need just Sunday at church but live Christ every day of the week, who do not need a specific place but only to be together, two or three, to have Christ with us (and I am not the one saying it, the Bible is).

One single building, not a thousand, not made of human constructions but of people who build up others who are not yet part of the completion of this great church. These bricks die, they need to be replaced, and that is why knowledge must be handed down. Knowledge sets you free (it is written in the Bible); it is not written that "hearsay" brings knowledge, nor that the Sunday sermon brings knowledge, but whoever reads the Bible grows in knowledge. The first times you read it, it will surely raise doubts, but the Holy Spirit will certainly fill that ignorance. It is not a man who will fill it, but the Holy Spirit (we only remember Him when we are told the little tale that Jesus, before ascending to heaven, said He would not leave us alone but would send the Holy Spirit).

Yes, the Holy Spirit, that conscience that tells you what is good and what is evil, that manna that comes to you in times of difficulty, and so on and so on, not that preacher who humanly suggests a study to you.

No, I am not angry with anyone and I have nothing against anyone, but to make the concept clear you have to be clear. A man does not teach another man. The Holy Spirit gives wisdom and understanding, and to know the Holy Spirit you need to know the Bible, recognise Jesus, accept Him into your life and always refer to Him and Him alone.

Have you ever read a book? No! Well, think of the Bible as many books, a library to read (that is exactly the translation: Bible = books).
It is hard to read, at times tiring, perhaps at times incomprehensible, and believe me, the first time, inexplicable. I assure you that the second time will be a memory of the first reading, but from the third time on it will become a real relationship with, or reference to, your own life, and an endless amount of knowledge that comes only from simply reading it.

If you don't believe me, just put yourself to the test; if you are someone who knows how to challenge yourself, just start.
That is how I started, and thanks to my challenge today I can tell you that if someone wants to teach me something about life, the Bible has already done it, and Christ continues to be present in my days.

Hi, I'm Solli, a brick exposed to the storms of this life who, joined to Christ and to other bricks like me scattered across this World, makes up the one, true church of God on earth.

Welcome. If you want to be shaped as a support of this building, start your challenge and be just as edifying with those who need biblical culture: read it, and get others to read it, for the Truth, the Way and the Life.
